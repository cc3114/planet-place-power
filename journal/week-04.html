Madrid's Vegetation

The question

What constitutes the greenness of three major urban‑peri‑urban green spaces in Madrid, and to what extent can NDVI reliably represent their real‑world habitat quality?

The data

Data comes from Esri Living Atlas public Multispectral Landsat service, sensor Landsat 9, spatial resolution 30 m per pixel. The scene was acquired 13 July 2026, cloud‑cover of the scene not provided in sampled metadata. This imagery service uses token‑free open licensing and allows anonymous browser access without ArcGIS organisational‑subscription. The calculated index is NDVI, computed from Band 4 (Red, visible red light, ~0.64‑0.67 μm) and Band 5 (Near‑Infrared NIR, ~0.85‑0.88 μm). NDVI Raw processing template was used to retrieve raw index values ranging −1 to 1 instead of colour‑table encoded output from NDVI Colorized. Sampled pixel values: Retiro Park: Band4 = 0.039, Band5 = 0.374, NDVI = 0.8111 Madrid Río Park: Band4 = 0.068, Band5 = 0.407, NDVI = 0.7137 Casa de Campo: Band4 = 0.132, Band5 = 0.293, NDVI = 0.3788

The method

Within ArcGIS Online Map Viewer I opened the saved tutorial map and added the Multispectral Landsat imagery layer from Living‑Atlas. I switched the processing template first to NDVI Colorized for visualisation and then to NDVI Raw to obtain real numeric NDVI pixel readings. Following I set an index threshold of 0.5 in Esri Landsat Explorer. Three sampling pixels were manually selected within Retiro Park, Madrid Río Park and Casa de Campo respectively. I recorded Band4, Band5 values and calculated NDVI manually for cross‑verification. Values come from the default mosaic scene for 13 July 2026.

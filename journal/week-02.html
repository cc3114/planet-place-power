Madrid's Temperature And Precipitaiton

The question

How does the temperature in the Madrid downtown area compare with the Madrid countryside?

The data

Esri Earth Observations. (2026, March 12). World Historical Climate - Monthly Averages for GHCN-D Stations for 1981 - 2010 [Feature layer]. ArcGIS Living Atlas. Retrieved September 10, 2026, from NOAA NCEI GHCN-Daily v3.24 (Menne et al., 2012).https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=ed59d3b4a8c44100914458dd722f054f#overview

The method

I used the Filter feature in ArcGIS to select the data. First, I set the condition to “All of the following are true”, and set the field to GHCN-D ID so as to search by the weather stations’ country and ID. Then I set the operator to “starts with”, and searched by the country’s abbreviated letters "SP". After locating the target country, I zoomed in and found the specific city Madrid, on which I conducted my research. By pointing at the colored station point, I got access to the station ID SPE00120287. By doing so, I set another condition connected with the first one by AND: field set to GHCN-D ID, operator "is", and value "SPE00120287". With the layer selected, I clicked "Configure charts" and added two Bar charts to the map. For the first chart, I set Category to Principal Station Name, set Aggregation to Mean, and added all twelve [Month] Avg. Temp C fields, January through December, in order. Similarly, for the second chart, I selected all twelve [Month] Mean Prcp mm fields.

What I found

The region near the equator is the warmest place in the world, and the dry belts are located around 30° in both hemispheres, where the most famous deserts such as the Sahara, Arabian, and Australian deserts are situated. This occurs because the sinking air at 30°, which originates from the equator, is dry air, causing both the atmosphere and the local environment to become hot and dry. Conversely, the wet belts are located around the equator and in subpolar regions. Around the equator, warm ocean temperatures induce heavy rainfall; around 50°–60°, warm, moist westerlies from the subtropics meet cold, dry easterlies from the poles, resulting in a persistent rainy season. Madrid consistently experiences a more extreme temperature range than coastal Spanish cities like Barcelona, which I attribute to its high elevation and surrounding mountain barriers that block moderating marine influences. Globally, most climate regions feature a summer-rainfall pattern where peak precipitation coincides with high temperatures. However, Madrid's Mediterranean climate is a distinct exception, characterized by hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. Situated in the Mediterranean zone, Madrid features a unique climate driven primarily by the seasonal north-south migration of the subtropical high-pressure belt and the westerlies. The weather stations available in Madrid are unevenly distributed. Because I aim to compare the diverse weather conditions across different parts of the city, this sparse distribution may not be precise enough to draw a definitive conclusion.

What this does not show

Regarding limitations, the greenhouse effect is rapidly altering global weather conditions. Since the project's dataset is based on the 1981–2010 baseline window, it may be somewhat outdated, ultimately leading to results that are less representative of current conditions.

Water

The question

Before this location became Casa de Campo public urban park, this land was royal hunting grounds on the western periphery of Madrid. In recent years between 2017 and 2025, the land‑cover change captured by the Sentinel‑2 dataset shows a reduction in tree‑class pixels alongside an increase in built‑area pixels within the park boundary. This assignment investigates the spatial pattern of these shifts and the uneven distribution of tree and built‑area labels across the site, while questioning whether these measured trends reflect real‑world park modification or artefacts from the land‑cover classifier. Local Madrid residents are the primary intended beneficiaries of this historic public green space.

The data

Sentinel‑2 10 m Land Use/Land Cover Time Series, produced by Esri and Impact Observatory, spatial resolution 10 m. The two comparison years are 2017 and 2025, generated using Esri MLA classification, source data under CC BY 4.0. This journal also uses the 1984–1992 Landsat layer at 30 m resolution for Part 3 historical comparison; this older layer carries no CC BY licence clause.

The method

Held constant the map extent, native 1:1 scale (10 m pixel), and land cover colour rendering across both dates. I locked the view frame after centring it on my study coordinate and did not pan or zoom it again. I included the reproducible app link above. I used 2019 as the third test date to validate the observed trends. From the change panel, I recorded six percentage change values: Water: 0%, Trees: −0.95%, Flooded Vegetation: 0%, Crops: −0.1%, Built Area: +0.4%, Bare Ground: +9%. You can use the link below to reproduce my work. https://livingatlas.arcgis.com/landcoverexplorer/#mapCenter=-3.717,40.417,14&mode=swipe&timeExtent=2017,2025&month=9

What I found

Within the fixed screen extent, the proportion of pixels labelled Built Area decreased slightly from 2017 to 2025, while Built Area pixels increased. I suspect a gradual tree canopy thinning and renewal of park pedestrian paths, but this subtle on‑ground vegetation and infrastructure change cannot be reliably shown by this classification dataset.

What this does not show

I am using the Sentinel‑2 Trees class to stand for the continuous tree canopy of this Madrid urban park site, and it might fail because the classifier’s category definition treats managed vegetated city parks as Built Area. Mixed 10‑metre pixels combining paths, lawn and trees may randomly flip class labels and create false change unrelated to actual vegetation conditions.

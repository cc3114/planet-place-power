Madrid's Terrain

The question

Where does water flow across the relief of western Madrid, and how do the overall landscape pattern, spatial scale, and drainage density dictate surface runoff towards the Manzanares basin?

The data

The elevation analysis uses the Spain_MadridCity_DTM1m dataset, accessed via Esri Terrain ImageServer under the ArcGIS Online Organizational Subscription Terms of Use. It features a 1 m cell size, an LE90 of 0.25 m, uses the vertical datum Alicante, and was flown between 12/5/2008 and 12/31/2012. The soil property analyzed is Soil Organic Carbon Density at a depth interval of 15–30 cm, sourced from SoilGrids (ISRIC) under a CC BY 4.0 license, and accessed via Esri Living Atlas under the Esri Master License Agreement.

The method

I set up the symbology to get slope from the elevation grid. I used a 3×3 (nine-cell) neighborhood window. In the slope degree window, I first showed elevation values with a continuous stretch. Because Madrid is formed as a plateau terrain and elevation does not change much within the city, I minimize the intervals of each range so as to highlight slope changes. I also used a custom color ramp. I set the transparency of the slope map to 34% and blended the layer with the multi-directional hillshade layer. This gave me a clear 3D surface.

What I found

Madrid generally slopes from the northwest and west toward the southeast and east, so when it rains, surface water flows eastward and southeastward into the Manzanares River.Material would move downslope from the higher western and northwestern areas toward the lower eastern and southeastern parts of the city. It would ultimately be deposited along the Manzanares River valley and its floodplain, and eventually carried into the Jarama River system. The city center has very little organic matter in the soil, which slowly increases as you move out toward the suburbs.

What this does not show

While the elevation model provides a fine 1 m cell size, it may still fail to capture extremely micro-scale slope anomalies, localized street tree pits, or slight construction variations at the site level. Additionally, the analysis is limited by the vertical datum it chose for that could introduce height errors when comparing this site to other datasets. Also, subscription-restricted policies in ArcGIS Online will let users who are not logged in may only see static images rather than interactive maps. AI use: AI-assisted for grammar refinement.

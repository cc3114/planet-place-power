Madrid's urban temperature is higher than rural's

The question

In Madrid, densely populated historic blocks and lower summer surface temperatures go together, because narrow street canyons provide mutual building shading while blocking afternoon convective heat, and I could check it with the urban-epoch layer against satellite summer surface heat.

The data

This analysis uses the "Summer Surface Heat" and "Population Density" layers from Site Scanner, both updated in 2026 by Google Earth Engine Apps. However, these datasets lack detailed building age histories and elevation profiles.

I am using Population Density to stand for urban built-up intensity, and it might fail because high population density alone does not necessary represents the administrative division. I am using Summer Surface Heat to stand for human thermal comfort, and it might fail because satellite-derived surface radiation differs from actual ground-level air temperature.

The method

I selected the 2026 Summer Surface Heat layer and overlaid the 2026 Population Density layer. I set the view to a city-wide zoom level across Madrid to evaluate how spatial surface heat aligns with concentrated human settlement.

What I found

The image supports a clear spatial relationship in Madrid where areas of high population density in the historic core exhibit lower summer surface heat than adjacent rural zones. The spatial distribution of heat shows that cooler thermal signatures are tightly tied to the clustering of pre-1900 dense urban blocks rather than peripheral mid-century developments.

What this does not show

This map does not account for micro-topography or elevation differences, nor does it capture the exact historical sequence of building construction across districts. I assumed that daytime building shading, narrow street canyons, and traditional construction materials drive this thermal reduction. If I zoomed out one step to the regional scale, the urban core's cool effect will differ base on other factors such as air- conditioner distribution.

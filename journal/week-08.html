Week 08 · October 2026

Land cover, then and now

The question

This investigation explores invisible digital and energy infrastructure across Madrid, Spain, examining how networked infrastructure appears on different spatial datasets at urban scale, and how place shapes what geospatial databases can capture. I compare visible electrical infrastructure and hidden telecom and data centre infrastructure to test the limits of crowdsourced and commercial geospatial records.

The method

I used Overpass Turbo, with responding server: gall.openstreetmap.de. Bounding box: the administrative extent of Madrid, Spain. Overpass query: [out:json][timeout:60]; nwr["telecom"]({{bbox}}); out center; [out:json][timeout:120]; ( nwr["telecom"]({{bbox}}); nwr["building"="data_center"]({{bbox}}); ); out center; The predicate filters for objects tagged telecom, power=tower, power=substation, and building=data_center within the bounding box of Madrid. I exported the power infrastructure results as GeoJSON, then imported the GeoJSON into ArcGIS Online. I manually added notes about terrestrial fibre interconnection from TeleGeography, as raw cable data could not be downloaded. I created a map visualisation and added required attribution and copyright statements in the map caption.

What I found

The OSM dataset captures Madrid’s overhead electrical infrastructure very well, as transmission towers and substations are visible from aerial imagery and public roads. However, the same crowdsourced database underrepresents telecom infrastructure. Many fibre routes are invisible to volunteers, so they remain unrecorded. While Madrid is a major terrestrial network hub for transatlantic internet traffic, it contains no submarine cable landing station. All undersea cables land on Spain’s coastline and connect inland via terrestrial fibre to Madrid’s data centre clusters. Data Center Map documents 84 operational data centres across Madrid, a much larger count than the building=data_center entries found in OSM. Also, if I re-run this query on a different Overpass mirror server, the total feature count may differ slightly, because mirrors refresh their OSM database snapshots at different intervals. This is a form of data snapshot inconsistency, comparable to the Week7 Atlas mismatch between webpage view and downloaded dataset.

What this does not show

I use OSM tagged telecom and building=data_center records to represent Madrid’s real-world telecom and data centre infrastructure. This proxy may fail because OSM contributors rely on publicly visible or aerial-view features. Underground fibre and locked private telecom buildings are hard to detect and are systematically under-recorded in the database.
